1. Platform/gateway commission gap. Ledger says the full sale amount; bank shows a smaller net amount because a payment processor or marketplace took a cut before settling. (This is the Zomato-shaped case, generalized — no platform name required, just "the settled amount is a plausible percentage of the invoiced amount.")

2. Flat transaction fee. A small, fixed amount is deducted — not a percentage, a flat number, same every time regardless of transaction size. Different mechanism than case 1, and your agent needs to tell them apart (percentage-of-amount vs. flat-number reasoning are genuinely different checks).

3. GST-inclusive vs. GST-exclusive mismatch. Ledger records a pre-tax amount; the real settled amount includes tax. You've already worked this one out in detail — it's a clean, explainable, non-alarming mismatch, and it's specifically an India/GST-relevant case, which matters given who's running this buildathon.

4. Settlement delay (T+1, T+2). Money doesn't land the same day it's invoiced — batched settlement is completely normal in payments, and your agent needs a date window, not an exact-date requirement, or it'll falsely flag every single normal transaction as unmatched.

5. Partial refund. Full amount was invoiced, but the customer got some money back, so the net settled amount is smaller — for a completely different reason than commission (this one should show a specific, not-a-percentage, not-a-round-number reduction, since refunds are usually item-specific, not proportional).
